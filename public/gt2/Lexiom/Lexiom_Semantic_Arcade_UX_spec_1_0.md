# 1. Semantic Guiding Principle

In Lexiom, legal work is not only procedural — it is semantic.

Conflict (and legal complexity) emerges from **incompatible positions**.  
Resolution emerges from **clarified interests** and **constructive language**.

The semantic engine is designed to:

- Translate **positions** into **interests**.
- Reduce emotional volatility **without erasing meaning**.
- Preserve authorship of each White player.
- Produce mutually intelligible language, suitable for legal artifacts.

Black acts as a **semantic bridge**, not a judge.

**Zenith note:** In solo play, “conflict” may be internal (uncertainty, risk, competing legal strategies). 
The same semantic ladder applies: position → interest → constructive proposal.

---

# 2. Core Semantic Layers

Each meaningful statement may exist in one of three semantic layers:

1. **Position Layer** — what a party declares they want.
2. **Interest Layer** — why they want it (underlying need / desire / value ).
3. **Constructive Proposal Layer** — forward-looking formulation that can satisfy multiple interests.

Goal of progression:

**Position → Interest → Constructive Proposal**

**Accord note:** The shared board prefers Interest and Constructive Proposal layers over raw positions, unless a position is explicitly published by a White.

---

# 3. Semantic Lifecycle of an Action Item

Every Action Item implicitly carries semantic state, and may evolve across layers.

## 3.1 Position-Centered Action Item

Examples:
- “Refund full amount.”
- “Admit wrongdoing.”

Black may propose reframed variants (draft-only).

## 3.2 Interest-Revealing Action Item

Examples:
- “Clarify financial impact and reimbursement expectations.”
- “Clarify accountability expectations and reputational concerns.”

These formulations move the case toward interests.

## 3.3 Constructive Proposal Action Item

Examples:
- “Draft structured repayment plan aligned with both parties’ constraints.”
- “Draft acknowledgment statement preserving dignity for both sides.”

**Temporal alignment:** Action Items evolve only through explicit White Moves (approve/edit/publish). Nothing silently “upgrades” its semantic layer.

---

# 4. Black’s Semantic Role

Black operates as an adaptive-language mediator.

Black may:
- Reframe aggressive phrasing into neutral form.
- Replace accusation verbs with descriptive verbs.
- Convert blame statements into problem statements.
- Suggest symmetry in language.
- Detect escalation signals and lower intensity.
- Offer alternative formulations across the three semantic layers.

Black may not:
- Alter meaning without user approval.
- Publish semantic transformations automatically.
- Attribute intent not expressed in canonical state.
- Introduce implied admissions or liability.

**Draft-only rule:** All reframing is draft-only until explicitly approved by a White.

**Accord alignment:** Black cannot write to shared harmony. Only Whites can publish.

---

# 5. L2 Topic Trio — Semantic Lenses

The three L2 topics are interpretive lenses (a “semantic camera” for the Center Playfield):

- **Clarification lens** — define what is being claimed, asked, denied, or requested.
- **Impact lens** — articulate consequences, constraints, harms, costs, risk, dignity.
- **Resolution lens** — shape options, tradeoffs, sequencing, mutual gains.

Selecting an L2 topic shifts the Center Playfield into that semantic mode.

**Spatial alignment:** L2 lives in the Top HUD; it always renders activity in the Center Playfield.

**Zenith note:** In solo mode, L2 lenses still apply; the “other party” may be the court, the opposing counsel, the client, or the user’s own uncertainty.

---

# 6. L3 Ribbon — Semantic Acceleration

Each L3 statement is a compact semantic move that accelerates meaning-shaping.

Examples:
- “Reframe toward shared impact.”
- “Clarify underlying expectations.”
- “Propose structured next step.”

Clicking an L3 statement:
- is an explicit White commit (a Move),
- commits to a semantic direction,
- triggers recalculation and new draft proposals.

**Spatial alignment:** L3 is always visible and centered; it is a fast “arcade lever,” not a hidden setting.

---

# 7. Shared Harmony Layer Semantics (Accord)

Published shared artifacts must follow shared-board semantic rules:

- Neutral descriptive tone.
- Avoid accusatory framing.
- Prefer bilateral phrasing.
- Avoid zero-sum wording.
- Make interests explicit when possible.
- Preserve dignity and procedural safety.

Optional cultural tone: “Thanks to ___ for ___” entries reinforce constructive contribution.

**Provenance alignment:** Shared items are immutable Moves in the ledger (full export includes accepted + unaccepted published moves).

**Zenith note:** In solo mode, this panel is intentionally quiet; shared-harmony rules apply only after upgrading into Accord.

---

# 8. Stage-Specific Semantic Modes

Lexiom calibrates semantic suggestions by stage.

## 8.1 Accord Stage Ladder (Shared)
- **Preparation** → fact gathering tone
- **Opening** → clarification of positions
- **Exposition** → expression of interests
- **Reframing** → language transformation
- **Proposals** → option generation
- **Resolutions** → agreement articulation

Black may recommend stage transitions; Whites must commit/publish/accept per the Temporal + Provenance rules.

## 8.2 Zenith Stage (Solo)
Zenith uses a single stage label: **Zenith** — a unified solo stage that contains the same semantic arc internally:

Preparation → Clarify → Reveal Interests → Reframe → Propose → Resolve

**Spatial alignment:** In Zenith, the Stage Tower presents only “Zenith” (solo cabinet). The same semantic calibration logic applies, but without shared-board constraints.

---

# 9. Semantic Safety Rules

Lexiom MUST enforce semantic safety constraints:

- No forced confession semantics.
- No implied liability without explicit approval.
- No escalation mirroring.
- No sarcasm replication.
- No absolute language without confirmation.
- No “mind-reading” attribution of intent.

High-impact transformations require explicit White approval.

**Accord note:** Shared-board publishing should prefer lower-risk formulations (Interest/Constructive Proposal), unless the user explicitly publishes a Position statement.

---

# 10. Semantic Determinism

Given identical canonical states and commits:

- Structural semantic category (Position / Interest / Proposal) must be deterministic.
- Draft variability is bounded by semantic rules, stage, and selected lens.

No hidden narrative bias.  
No invisible updates between rounds.

---

# 11. Semantic Calmness Principle

Psychological calmness emerges when:

- Meaning is clarified progressively.
- Language becomes symmetrical.
- Interests become visible.
- Proposals become concrete.
- Users retain authorship and control.

The system optimizes for **mutual intelligibility**, not victory.

**Zenith note:** Calmness also means: the user can explore alternatives safely, without committing them to record until they explicitly approve/publish.

---

# 12. Mastery Curve (Arcade Semantics)

Easy to learn:
- Click a topic lens.
- Approve a reframing.
- Select a semantic direction (L3).

Difficult to master:
- Choosing the right semantic shift at the right moment.
- Moving from position to interest without losing dignity.
- Proposing solutions reflecting multiple constraints.
- Publishing only what should enter the shared record.

Fast moves. Clear consequences. Refined language.
