## Case Seed Narrative

### Background

Our client, a tenant, vacated the rental premises on March 1. Upon moving out, our client documented the condition of the property through photographs and a walkthrough email. Despite these efforts, the landlord is withholding the security deposit of $3,000, asserting that damages were incurred that exceed ordinary wear and tear.

### Landlord's Position

In a response to our client's inquiries, the landlord disputes the amount of the security deposit, claiming it was $2,000 rather than $3,000. The landlord further asserts that an inspection conducted after the tenant's departure revealed multiple issues requiring repair and cleaning, specifically citing damage to the wooden kitchen. The landlord contends that these damages justify the withholding of the deposit and states that they are in the process of gathering invoices and estimates to substantiate their claims.

### Legal Obligations

The lease agreement stipulates that the security deposit must be returned within 30 days of the tenant's move-out date. Given that our client has not received the deposit within this timeframe, and considering the landlord's failure to substantiate the alleged damages with documented evidence, our client seeks to initiate a demand for the return of the security deposit. Should the landlord continue to withhold the funds, our client is prepared to pursue a small claims action to recover the amount owed.

### Conclusion

In light of the above facts and the landlord's obligations under the lease agreement, a demand letter will be drafted to formally request the return of the security deposit. If necessary, further legal action will be considered to ensure our client's rights are upheld.
