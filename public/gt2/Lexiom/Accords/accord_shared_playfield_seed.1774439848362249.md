Security Deposit Dispute

## Common Ground
Both parties acknowledge that the landlord is withholding the tenant's $2,000 security deposit. It is also agreed that the lease stipulates the deposit must be returned within 30 days of the tenant's move-out date, which was March 1. Furthermore, the tenant has made multiple refund requests that have not received a response from the landlord.

## Clarifiable Differences
The primary point of contention lies in the landlord's claims of damages allegedly caused by the tenant, which the tenant disputes. The landlord may argue that the deposit is justified due to these alleged damages, while the tenant maintains that they are not responsible for any such damages. Additionally, the landlord might assert that he has responded to the refund requests, whereas the tenant insists on a lack of communication.

## Undisputed Anchors
- The landlord is withholding the security deposit.
- The lease states the deposit must be returned within 30 days.
- The tenant moved out on March 1.
- The landlord has ignored refund requests.
- The tenant did not take photos upon moving out.

## Invitation Direction
In the spirit of collaboration, it is encouraged that both parties engage in open communication to clarify the situation regarding the deposit and the alleged damages. A constructive dialogue can help address misunderstandings and work towards a resolution that is satisfactory for both the landlord and the tenant.
