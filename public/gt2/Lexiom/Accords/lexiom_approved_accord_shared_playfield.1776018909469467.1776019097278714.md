### Security Deposit Dispute

## Common Ground
Both parties acknowledge that the tenant vacated the rental premises on March 1. Furthermore, the lease agreement explicitly states that the security deposit must be returned within 30 days of the tenant vacating the property. This provision establishes a clear timeline for the return of the security deposit.

## Clarifiable Differences
1. The tenant asserts that the security deposit was $3,000, while the landlord claims it was $2,000. This discrepancy requires clarification and substantiation from both parties.
2. The landlord alleges that damages to the property necessitate repairs beyond normal wear and tear, specifically citing issues with the wooden kitchen and other items. The tenant disputes this characterization and contends that the condition of the premises was acceptable upon vacating.
3. The landlord is in the process of gathering invoices and estimates to support claims of damages, which may need to be reviewed to assess their validity.

## Undisputed Anchors
- The tenant vacated the rental premises on March 1.
- The lease agreement stipulates that the security deposit must be returned within 30 days of vacating the property.

## Invitation Direction
In light of the existing disputes, it is advisable for both parties to engage in a constructive dialogue to resolve the matter amicably. The tenant is prepared to send a demand letter for the return of the security deposit and is open to discussing potential resolutions prior to pursuing formal legal action. This approach aims to foster a collaborative environment that respects the interests of both parties while adhering to the terms of the lease agreement.
