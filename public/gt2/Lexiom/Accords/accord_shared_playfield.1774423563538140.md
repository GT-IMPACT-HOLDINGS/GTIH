Security Deposit Dispute

## Common Ground
Both parties acknowledge that the landlord is withholding a $2000 security deposit, and the lease explicitly states that the deposit must be returned within 30 days of the tenant moving out. The tenant moved out on March 1, which establishes a clear timeline for the landlord's obligations. Additionally, the landlord's lack of communication regarding refund requests is recognized by the tenant.

## Clarifiable Differences
The primary dispute lies in the landlord's claims of damages allegedly caused by the tenant, which the tenant disputes. While the landlord may present evidence to support their claims, the tenant asserts that they are not responsible for any damages. Furthermore, the landlord may argue that they addressed the tenant's refund requests, while the tenant feels these requests were ignored. The interpretation of the lease terms regarding the conditions for withholding the deposit is also a point of contention.

## Undisputed Anchors
- The landlord is withholding the $2000 security deposit.
- The lease states that the deposit must be returned within 30 days.
- The tenant moved out on March 1.
- The landlord is ignoring refund requests.
- The tenant did not take photos upon moving out.

## Invitation Direction
In the spirit of collaboration, it is important for both parties to engage in open communication to resolve this matter amicably. The tenant seeks a clear acknowledgment of their position regarding the deposit and any alleged damages, while the landlord is encouraged to provide any evidence they may have. A constructive dialogue can lead to a satisfactory resolution for both parties.
