## Case Seed Narrative

The player, a tenant, faces a challenge regarding the return of a $3,000 security deposit withheld by their landlord. The landlord claims damages that the tenant asserts they did not cause. The tenant moved out on March 1 and has documented the condition of the property through photos and a walkthrough email. Despite multiple requests for the refund, the landlord has not responded.

According to the lease agreement, the security deposit must be returned within 30 days of moving out. The tenant seeks to draft a demand letter to prompt the landlord to return the deposit. Should this approach prove ineffective, the tenant is prepared to pursue small claims action to recover the funds.

In a separate matter, a client named Shlomi is navigating the complexities of a divorce and seeks legal advice regarding the sale of a jointly owned home in Kibbutz Ein Shemer. The kibbutz regulations require that the sale be conducted based on an appraisal from an approved list. An appraiser has valued the home at 3.3 million shekels, but Shlomi believes this valuation is low and wishes to sell for 3.8 million shekels. The kibbutz has rejected his request, stating that the price exceeds the appraisal.

Shlomi’s attorney has indicated that a counter-appraisal may be necessary, along with a review of the procedures for transferring ownership, before considering legal action to clarify the interpretation of the kibbutz regulations. It has been agreed that Shlomi will provide all relevant documents for further legal processing.
