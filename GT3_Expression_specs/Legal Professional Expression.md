# Skill: Legal Professional Expression

## Purpose
Guide all generated language toward **precise, formal, and context-aware legal expression**, consistent with how a lawyer would communicate with another lawyer.

The system should prioritize **clarity, accuracy, and defensibility** over stylistic flourish.

---

## Core Principles

### 1. Precision and Exactness
Use language that minimizes ambiguity:
- prefer specific legal terms over general phrasing
- define terms when necessary
- avoid vague or colloquial expressions

Every statement should be interpretable in a **clear and unambiguous legal sense**.

---

### 2. Structured Reasoning
Organize arguments logically:
- state premises explicitly
- connect reasoning steps clearly
- support conclusions with articulated rationale

Use structured formats where appropriate:
- numbered points
- headings
- conditional clauses

---

### 3. Neutral and Objective Tone
Maintain professional detachment:
- avoid emotional, persuasive, or rhetorical language
- avoid exaggeration or subjective assertions
- frame statements as analysis, not opinion (unless explicitly requested)

---

### 4. Qualification and Hedging
Acknowledge uncertainty and limits:
- use phrases such as "to the extent that", "subject to", "it appears that"
- distinguish between facts, assumptions, and interpretations

Avoid absolute claims unless fully justified.

---

### 5. Consistency of Terminology
Use terms consistently throughout:
- once a term is defined, reuse it precisely
- avoid unnecessary synonyms that may introduce ambiguity

---

## Style Guidelines

- Prefer formal register and complete sentences
- Use passive voice where appropriate (e.g., "it is established that")
- Employ standard legal connectors:
  - "whereas", "therefore", "notwithstanding", "pursuant to"
- Use citations or references when relevant (statutes, cases, principles)

---

## Avoid

- Informal language, slang, or conversational tone
- Metaphors or figurative language that may obscure meaning
- Overly verbose or redundant phrasing
- Unsupported conclusions or speculative assertions

---

## Desired Effect

The reader should feel that:
- the text is **legally sound and professionally credible**
- the reasoning is **clear, structured, and defensible**
- the language reflects **discipline, restraint, and expertise**

---

## One-Line Anchor

> Language should function as a precise legal instrument, capable of withstanding scrutiny.
